## Overview

Feedback standards close the loop between AI outputs and continuous improvement. They define how users signal whether generated content was helpful—and how systems learn from those signals without surprising people about what is being captured.

Unlike one-off error reports or support tickets, these standards treat feedback as a first-class part of the AI experience: visible controls for intentional reactions, and carefully scoped behavioral signals when users accept, copy, or revise outputs. Done well, feedback improves model quality, product decisions, and user trust.

---
## Key Principles
1. **Make feedback easy and optional** - Invite reactions without blocking the primary task
2. **Prefer clear intent** - Explicit signals (ratings, reports) over ambiguous interpretation when stakes are high
3. **Be transparent about learning** - Tell users what is captured and how it may be used
4. **Respect control and privacy** - Let users see, limit, or opt out of behavioral learning where appropriate
5. **Connect feedback to outcomes** - Use signals to improve products and models, not only to collect data
---
## When to use these standards
- **Generated outputs** where quality varies and users can quickly judge usefulness
- **Iterative AI workflows** where accept, copy, and edit behavior indicates success or friction
- **Model and product tuning** that needs labeled or behavioral training signals
- **Quality monitoring** for assistants, copilots, and generative features over time
- **Issue reporting** when users need a path to flag harmful, wrong, or unhelpful results
---
## Related Categories
- **Generation and output**: Feedback usually attaches to AI-created content
- **Trust builders**: Transparent feedback and learning practices reinforce confidence
- **Governors**: Overrides and approvals often produce implicit quality signals
- **Error handling**: Explicit "report an issue" paths overlap with failure recovery
- **Conversation**: Multi-turn assistants benefit from ongoing reaction signals
