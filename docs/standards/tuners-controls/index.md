## Overview

Tuners & Controls standards give users agency over AI behavior without requiring deep technical knowledge. Unlike traditional software settings, AI controls often involve trade-offs between competing values (speed vs. quality, creativity vs. consistency). These standards help users understand and navigate those trade-offs through clear labels, smart defaults, and progressive disclosure.

Well-designed tuners empower users to customize AI behavior for their specific context while protecting them from overwhelming complexity. They make the system adaptable without sacrificing usability.

---
## Key Principles
1. **Agency without expertise** - Users can shape AI behavior without needing technical knowledge
2. **Make trade-offs visible** - Surface choices like speed vs. quality and creativity vs. consistency
3. **Start with smart defaults** - Provide a good first experience, then let people tune
4. **Progressive disclosure** - Reveal advanced controls as users need them
5. **Protect from overwhelm** - Complexity should stay optional, not required
---
## When to use these standards
- **Model or mode selection** where users choose how the AI should work
- **Parameter tuning** such as creativity, length, or formality
- **Filters and refinement** after generation, without starting over
- **Persistent preferences** like system instructions, memory, or connected services
---
## Related Categories
- **Input and authoring**: Controls often sit alongside how users write prompts
- **Generation and output**: Tuning changes what users see in results
- **Conversation**: Mode switching often happens inside a chat
- **Foundations**: Ethical limits still apply when users adjust behavior
