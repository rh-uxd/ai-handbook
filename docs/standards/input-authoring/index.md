## Overview

Input & Authoring standards address one of the most critical challenges in AI UX: helping users communicate their intent effectively. Whether through natural language prompts, structured forms, or multimodal inputs, these standards bridge the gap between what users want to express and what AI systems can understand.

Unlike traditional form inputs, AI systems often accept open-ended, conversational, or creative inputs. These standards help users understand how to structure their requests, what level of detail to provide, and how to refine their inputs when results don't match expectations.

---
## Key Principles
1. **Lower the barrier to entry** - Make it easy to get started, even for novice users
2. **Provide scaffolding** - Offer structure without constraining creativity
3. **Show examples** - Demonstrate what good inputs look like
4. **Enable iteration** - Make it easy to refine and improve prompts
5. **Support different expertise levels** - Serve both beginners and power users
---
## When to use these standards
- **Open-ended AI interactions** where users need to craft prompts
- **Complex requests** that benefit from structured guidance
- **Creative tasks** where users need inspiration
- **Technical systems** where prompt quality significantly impacts results
- **Multi-step processes** where inputs build on each other
---
## Related Categories
- **Wayfinders**: Help users discover what kinds of inputs are possible
- **Tuners and controls**: Allow refinement of AI behavior after initial input
- **Conversation**: Many conversational standards involve iterative input refinement
- **Error handling**: Address what happens when inputs don't produce desired results
